Frequently asked questions

Straight answers about moving to New Zealand

The questions we hear most from people thinking about the move. If yours isn't here, ask us directly — the first conversation is free.

Getting started

Am I eligible to migrate to New Zealand? +
It depends on your age, qualifications, work experience, English ability, health and character — and which visa category you apply under. Rather than guessing, book a free 30-minute assessment and we'll tell you honestly which pathways are open to you, and which aren't.
Do I need a job offer before I can apply? +
For most work visas, yes — the Accredited Employer Work Visa requires an offer from an accredited New Zealand employer. Some residence pathways, however, can be built on qualifications and experience. We'll map which routes need an offer and which don't for your situation.
How long does the whole process take? +
Timeframes vary widely by visa type and Immigration New Zealand's current processing volumes. Work visas are often decided in weeks to a few months; residence applications can take considerably longer. In your strategy plan we give you a realistic timeline for your specific pathway, and we keep you updated as your application moves.
Can my partner and children come with me? +
In many cases, yes. Most work and residence pathways allow you to include or support a partner and dependent children, each with their own visa application. We plan the whole family's applications together so everyone's status lines up.

Working with PPIM

Why use a licensed adviser instead of applying myself? +
You can absolutely apply on your own. People come to us because immigration instructions change frequently, the evidence standards are unforgiving, and a declined application costs time, money and sometimes future options. A licensed adviser gets the strategy and the paperwork right the first time — and is legally accountable for the advice they give.
What does "IAA licensed" actually mean? +
In New Zealand it is illegal to give immigration advice unless you are licensed by the Immigration Advisers Authority (IAA) or are exempt, such as NZ lawyers. Licensing means our advisers meet ongoing competency standards, follow a professional code of conduct, and you have a formal complaints process if anything goes wrong.
How much do your services cost? +
It depends on the visa type and complexity of your case. What we promise: a fixed fee quoted in writing before any work begins, with no hourly billing and no surprise charges. Government application fees are always listed separately so you can see exactly where your money goes.
Can you help if I'm not in New Zealand yet? +
Yes — most of our clients are overseas when we start working together. Consultations run over video call across time zones, and documents are handled securely online. You don't need to set foot in New Zealand until your visa says you can.

Applications & outcomes

What happens if my application is declined? +
First, we work hard to make sure it isn't — we don't lodge applications we believe will fail. If a decline does happen, options can include responding to concerns, requesting reconsideration, appealing to the Immigration and Protection Tribunal, or reapplying under a stronger strategy. We'll advise you frankly on which is worth pursuing.
Will a medical condition or past conviction stop me? +
Not necessarily. New Zealand has health and character requirements, but waivers exist for many situations. The key is disclosing everything honestly and presenting it properly — concealment is far more damaging than the issue itself. Tell us early and we'll assess how it affects your options.
How do I eventually become a permanent resident or citizen? +
Residence is usually the first milestone. After holding residence and meeting time-in-country requirements, you can apply for permanent residence, and later for citizenship — generally after five years of living in New Zealand as a resident. We plan with the long game in mind from your very first visa.
